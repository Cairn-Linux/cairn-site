Why we're building it

A smaller beginning.
A real computer.

An adult desktop asks a young child to understand a lot at once. Cairn starts with a simple home screen and apps their parent has chosen. Making things and playing games give them a reason to explore.

As they become more confident, the plan is to introduce their own files, more commands, and eventually a regular desktop. They keep their account and familiar surroundings.

We are starting with children around five to eight, with a design that extends into the early teens. Parents choose access based on readiness, not age alone.

The experience we're working toward

Find something to do.
Find your way back.

These are design goals, not a claim that every flow is finished.

  1. Choose an app

    A clear home screen, readable names and room to use a mouse or keyboard. The adult chooses which apps are available.

  2. Make something, or play

    Existing drawing tools and games keep their own personalities. Cairn provides the way in and the way home.

  3. Try the terminal

    A small set of real commands lets children open things and learn how instructions work. Suggestions help when a word goes wrong.

  4. Recover without a scare

    A failed launch or frozen app should lead somewhere understandable. Recovery is part of the design, not an afterthought.

Where we are

A working prototype.
Not a finished release.

Read the working roadmap

The launcher and restricted terminal have working code. Test-machine setup and child/adult login routing have been exercised in a virtual machine. Initial game scanning and Steam-window containment work are in place.

Before a child trial, the remaining work includes:

  • Tightening child-account permissions and providing recovery from a frozen app.
  • Completing signed-in Steam checks and older-laptop testing.

Then comes the important test: watch a child use the full session and change what doesn't work. A public installer comes later.

A few honest answers

Can I install it for my child today?

There is no public installer. Developer provisioning exists for testing, but the prototype is not ready to recommend for everyday family use.

Will I manage it from my phone?

No. The design puts adult administration at the computer through a separate Guardian role. Cairn does not plan a remote management service.

Does it need accounts or an internet connection?

Cairn's design excludes project accounts, telemetry and analytics. Updates and app installation need connectivity. Optional services such as Steam and Minecraft can have their own accounts and network requirements.

Is it a replacement for adult supervision?

No. The goal is age-appropriate independence, with an adult choosing access. The prototype's restrictions are still being tested.

Will it work on our spare laptop?

The current minimum target is a 64-bit Intel or AMD laptop with 4 GB of memory, 64 GB of storage, UEFI and Intel HD 4000-class graphics or better. These are targets, not a compatibility guarantee. ARM and NVIDIA support are outside the current v1 scope.
